
I built and scaled NutriScope—a client-side AI Nutrition Analytics application—moving from a barebones MVP (Prompt 1) to a full-featured, interactive analytics platform (Prompt 2) using Claude.

Here is how the application transformed between the initial prototype and the enhanced production version:

🚀 What Changed: MVP vs. Enhanced Version

📂 Data Ingestion & Dataset Expansion:

MVP: Built with a hardcoded, 20-food baseline database.

Enhanced: Expanded to 60+ local and global food profiles, plus added a CSV Bulk Upload engine to ingest custom dataset logs seamlessly.

🧬 Advanced Micronutrients & Risk Analysis:

MVP: Tracked standard baseline macros and 5 basic micronutrients.

Enhanced: Deepened the analysis engine to include advanced micronutrient profiling, automated Deficiency & Excess Risk Analysis, and clinical educational disclaimers grounded in verified nutritional sources.

🗓️ Planner & Recommendation Engine:

MVP: Provided basic, static food swap suggestions based on general dietary preferences (Veg, Non-Veg, Eggetarian).

Enhanced: Upgraded to an interactive 2-Day Meal Planner with dynamic, personalized portion adjustments, intelligent food swaps, and contextual dietary recommendations.

📊 UX & Visual Analytics Upgrade:

MVP: Basic progress bars and a standard Chart.js macro breakdown.

Enhanced: Polished premium SaaS dark-mode UI featuring high-density modern cards, responsive multi-axis charting, and scannable visual indicators.

The Takeaway:
Iteration speed in generative application development comes down to structured prompting. By establishing a working baseline MVP first and then layering complex features (data input, risk models, multi-day state) sequentially, you can ship production-ready UI/UX in a fraction of the time.
