For Day 25 of the #60DayClaudeChallenge, I turned Claude into an interactive AI Shark Tank Simulator—a multi-agent pitch environment designed to challenge startup assumptions, grill unit economics, and test founder readiness in real time.

Building on the MVP validation and business strategy from Days 23 and 24, I wanted to see how well the concept held up when subjected to relentless, multi-persona investor pushback.

Here is how I engineered the multi-persona pitch simulator:

🎭 1. Multi-Investor Persona Prompting

Configured Claude to simulate distinct investor styles: the Margins & Unit Economics Hawk (focusing on CAC/LTV & token costs), the Technical Moat Skeptic (challenging wrapper defensibility), and the GTM Growth VC (grilling customer acquisition pipelines).

⚡ 2. Real-Time Pitch & Q&A Simulation

Delivered the initial elevator pitch, prompting the "Sharks" to fire back probing follow-up questions, flag hidden risks, and demand immediate justification for valuation and pricing.

📊 3. Term Sheet vs. Rejection Post-Mortem

Concluded the simulation with a structured evaluation report: listing deal/no-deal decisions, explicit valuation offers, critical weakness breakdowns, and strategic pitch refinements.

💡 The Key Takeaway:
Pitching isn't about memorizing slides; it's about handling edge-case pushback. Using AI as a multi-persona simulator lets you fail fast in a sandbox, uncover narrative blind spots, and refine your business model before stepping into a real room of investors.
